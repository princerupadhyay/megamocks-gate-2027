Day 29 of my GATE CSE 2027 prep · 12 October 2026

Six COA Lectures, a Full Workday, and Navratri Distractions

Feeling: steady, a little behind on DBMS, mildly annoyed by garba
Computer Organization and Architecture Database Management Systems

Monday was a reasonably productive day for my GATE CSE 2027 preparation. I managed to complete all six planned lectures of Computer Organization and Architecture (COA), although I couldn't fit in the additional DBMS lecture I had planned to recover from Sunday's backlog.

And, as if balancing a full-time job and GATE preparation wasn't enough, Navratri celebrations have brought another challenge into the picture: loud garba music in my society.

Here's how Day 29 went.

What I Studied Today: COA Basics

I started the first chapter of Computer Organization and Architecture, which covers the basics of COA.

Fortunately, this chapter wasn't entirely new to me. Most of the concepts felt familiar, so I could revise them comfortably without struggling to understand everything from scratch.

I covered the basics of registers, including different special-purpose registers such as the Instruction Register, address and data registers, and Program Counter.

I also revised different types of computer architectures, including register-based architecture, memory-based architecture, stack-based architecture, and a more complex architecture in which operands can be supplied from different locations.

The next topic was system buses.

I went through the three main types:

  • Address bus: Used to carry addresses and is generally unidirectional in the basic bus model.
  • Data bus: Carries data and is generally bidirectional.
  • Control bus: Carries control signals that coordinate different operations in the computer.

The control bus was the part that needed a little more attention. I understood the basic concept, but understanding how individual control lines carry different control signals was somewhat difficult to grasp fully.

Sometimes, reading a definition is easy, but understanding exactly how it works takes a little more effort. This was one of those moments.

Still, nothing in the chapter felt particularly exhausting because most of the material was revision for me. I could comfortably follow the lectures at around 2× to 2.25× speed.

By the end of the day, I had completed all six planned COA lectures.

Why I Couldn't Complete the DBMS Lecture

There was one more task on my list: completing a DBMS lecture that I had missed on Sunday.

Unfortunately, that remained incomplete.

Each lecture was approximately two hours long. Even when watching at 2× or 2.5× speed, completing one still requires a substantial amount of uninterrupted time. In practice, I had already spent roughly five to six hours watching my COA lectures.

On top of that, I had worked for around seven to eight hours at the office.

Trying to squeeze in another two-hour lecture after that was difficult. I simply couldn't find enough time to complete it.

I don't consider this a particularly mysterious productivity problem. The workload was already substantial, and the extra lecture required more time than I had available.

The important thing is that I completed my regular COA target. I didn't finish everything I had planned, but I also didn't abandon the day's main schedule.

I'm satisfied with that balance.

Navratri Has Added a New Challenge

There's another thing affecting my preparation this week.

Navratri celebrations are going on, and people in my society play garba with loud music. While it's a nice part of the festivities, the noise makes it difficult to concentrate on studying, especially late at night.

Because of this, I've been trying to avoid studying after dinner.

Instead of fighting the noise and repeatedly losing concentration, I plan to complete my heavier study sessions earlier and use the time after dinner for lighter tasks whenever possible.

The good thing is that this is a temporary situation. The celebrations will last only nine days, so I think I can manage these distractions with a few adjustments to my routine.

There's no need to redesign my entire preparation strategy because of a temporary disturbance. I'll work around it and return to my normal routine once things settle down.

Tuesday's Plan: COA Plus DBMS Catch-Up

My Tuesday schedule is already set.

I'll continue with the six COA lectures planned for the day. Alongside those, I'll try to complete one DBMS lecture from Sunday's backlog.

That makes seven planned lectures in total.

I know that adding another lecture isn't always easy when I'm already balancing office work and my regular study targets. But I'll try to fit it in without unnecessarily disturbing the rest of the schedule.

The plan is to recover the missed DBMS work gradually rather than shift the entire weekly schedule by a day.

Let's see how it goes.

What I Realized Today

Monday reminded me that planning a study schedule and executing it are two different things.

On paper, adding one extra lecture might look simple. But when that lecture requires a significant amount of time after an already demanding workday, the calculation changes.

At the same time, I don't want to keep postponing unfinished work indefinitely. I still have my October 30 syllabus-completion deadline to keep in mind, so I need to be careful about how I distribute my remaining lectures.

For now, I'm happy with what I accomplished. Six COA lectures are complete, the DBMS lecture is still pending, and I have a plan for Tuesday.

I'll also have to deal with the garba music for a few more days. Heavy studying before dinner and lighter work afterward seems like the most practical approach for now.

Day 29 wasn't perfect, but it was productive. Tomorrow, the target is six more COA lectures and one DBMS catch-up lecture.
