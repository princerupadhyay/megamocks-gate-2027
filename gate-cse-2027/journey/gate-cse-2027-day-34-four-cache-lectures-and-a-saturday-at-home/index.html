Day 34 of my GATE CSE 2027 prep · 17 October 2026

Four Cache Lectures and a Saturday at Home

Feeling: calm, at home, quietly thinking ahead
Computer Organization and Architecture Operating Systems

Saturday was a smooth day for my GATE CSE 2027 preparation. I completed four lectures of Cache Organization, prepared notes for all of them, and spent some time at home with my father.

There was nothing particularly dramatic about the day, and honestly, that was perfectly fine. The lectures went well, the concepts were familiar, and I was able to make the planned academic progress.

What I Studied Today: COA Cache Organization

My target for Saturday was to continue Cache Organization, a chapter from Computer Organization and Architecture (COA).

I completed lectures 4, 5, 6, and 7 and prepared notes alongside them.

One thing that made the chapter easier was its similarity to paging in Operating Systems. I had already studied paging, so some of the underlying ideas felt familiar. Although Cache Organization introduced new concepts, I wasn't approaching everything from scratch.

Because of that previous exposure, the lectures went smoothly. I was able to understand the material without encountering any major conceptual difficulties.

I also made notes for all four lectures, which should be useful when I begin revising the subject and solving more GATE questions.

The Problem of Remembering Everything

Even when a chapter feels easy during lectures, there is another challenge waiting ahead: remembering all the concepts when solving questions.

While thinking about the revision phase, I realised that GATE preparation involves an enormous number of small details. Sometimes, understanding a concept while watching a lecture feels straightforward, but remembering exactly when and how to apply it in a question is a different matter.

This is something I need to address during revision.

After my planned syllabus completion on 30 October, I will have November, December, and January available for revision and mock tests before the GATE CSE 2027 examination in February.

I'm still thinking about how to organise those three months effectively while managing my job. I want to revise the subjects properly, practise questions, take mock tests, and analyse my mistakes without letting everything become overwhelming.

I haven't finalised that strategy yet. I'll work out a chart and see how to organise the available time.

For now, my priority remains completing the syllabus as planned.

A Saturday at Home

Unlike Friday, I didn't have to travel anywhere on Saturday. It was the weekend, and I was at home.

I'm quite a homesick person. Whenever I get the opportunity, I prefer spending my weekends at home rather than staying away.

Being at home also gives me an opportunity to focus on my studies and career goals. Preparing for GATE while working isn't easy, so having time at home is something I value.

There's a difference between studying while being away from home and studying when you're actually in a familiar environment. At home, I feel that I have more time to focus on the things I want to achieve.

Of course, Saturday wasn't only about studying.

My father also had a holiday that day, so I spent some time with him. It was a simple personal moment, but it was worth including in my preparation diary.

I don't want this blog to become a list of lectures and completed chapters. My GATE preparation is happening alongside my actual life, and spending time with family is part of that life.

Tomorrow's Targets

For Sunday, I have planned another round of Cache Organization lectures.

My COA target is to complete lectures 8, 9, 10, and 11. Alongside that, I plan to start the DBMS Query Language chapter and cover lectures 1, 2, and 3.

There is one thing I still need to address: the DBMS backlog from the Sunday when I was struggling with allergic rhinitis.

That backlog is still pending.

So, while I have new lectures scheduled for Sunday, I also need to keep the older unfinished work in mind. I don't want to lose track of it while moving ahead with the syllabus.

My overall deadline remains 30 October, and I want to finish the syllabus without relying unnecessarily on the buffer days reserved for emergencies.

What I Realised Today

Today's progress was straightforward. I completed four lectures, made notes, and found the concepts easier to understand because of my previous exposure to paging in Operating Systems.

At the same time, I'm becoming more aware of the challenge that comes after syllabus completion: revision, question practice, and mock-test analysis.

Finishing lectures is one milestone. Being able to recall and apply those concepts under exam conditions is another.

I still have work to do before I reach that stage, but for now, Saturday went according to plan.

I also got to spend some time at home with my father, which made the day about more than just academics.

Day 34 complete. Four more Cache Organization lectures done, notes prepared, and another step taken towards completing the GATE CSE 2027 syllabus by 30 October.
