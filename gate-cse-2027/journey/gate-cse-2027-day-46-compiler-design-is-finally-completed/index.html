Day 46 of my GATE CSE 2027 prep · 29 October 2026

Compiler Design Is Finally Completed!

Feeling: accomplished, newly confident, mindful of pending notes
Compiler Design Database Management Systems

Thursday was a satisfying day in my GATE CSE 2027 preparation. I completed the remaining two lectures of Code Generation and Code Optimization, which was the final chapter of Compiler Design.

And with that, the entire Compiler Design lecture syllabus is officially completed!

What makes this completion a little more special is how I felt about this subject when I first started studying it.

Completing the Final Compiler Design Chapter

On Wednesday, I had completed the first two lectures of Code Generation and Code Optimization. Both lectures were surprisingly easy to follow.

My target for Thursday was straightforward: complete the remaining two lectures and finish the chapter.

Fortunately, things went just as planned.

I completed both lectures, and interestingly, the last two were just as easy as the first two. I didn't encounter any major conceptual difficulties.

Each lecture was approximately 2.5–3 hours long, so completing them was a decent amount of work.

With this, all the Compiler Design chapters have been covered.

There is still some unfinished work, though. I haven't prepared notes for any of the Compiler Design lectures yet. The lectures are completed, but the notes are still waiting for their turn.

DBMS is also pending, including its remaining work and notes.

So, while the lecture syllabus is complete, there is still some consolidation work left before I can consider the subject properly prepared for revision.

Compiler Design: From an Enemy to a Friend

This is probably the most interesting part of completing Compiler Design.

When I started this subject, I was genuinely concerned about it. I hadn't studied Compiler Design during either my B.Sc. or MCA, so almost everything was new to me.

As I progressed through the subject, I encountered topics like LL(1), LR(0), SLR(1), CLR, lookahead, parsing tables and several relationships between different parsing techniques.

Some of these concepts were genuinely difficult to understand.

There were moments when I wondered how I would manage such a subject, especially with the GATE syllabus containing so many other topics.

But after spending enough time with the subject, my perception started changing.

And today, I have a realization that I want to remember throughout my GATE preparation:

Every subject is easy when you spend enough time with it.

Of course, some subjects might be more difficult than others, and some topics will naturally require more effort. But judging a subject before becoming familiar with it doesn't really make sense.

If you want to understand a subject, you need to spend time with it.

You need to become familiar with its concepts, terminology, patterns and way of thinking.

Instead of treating a subject as your enemy, spend enough time with it to make it your friend.

This is something I experienced firsthand with Compiler Design.

A Change in Confidence

Completing Compiler Design has changed my confidence quite a lot.

Earlier, whenever I thought about starting an unfamiliar subject, there was some hesitation.

The thought of studying something completely new while managing multiple other GATE CSE subjects was intimidating.

But now I have a different perspective.

I don't necessarily need to know a subject beforehand to start studying it. I need to be willing to spend time understanding it.

That doesn't mean every subject will suddenly become easy or that I won't struggle with difficult concepts again.

But at least I have one more reason not to judge a subject too early.

And I think that's a valuable lesson to carry forward.

A Regular Day at the Office

Apart from studying, Thursday was a pretty normal office day.

Office work went on as usual, nothing particularly interesting happened there.

One small difference was during my return journey. I usually leave the office with Siddesh, but he had to leave early because of some work.

So, I came back home alone that day.

Nothing extraordinary, just one of those little everyday changes that happen during a regular working day.

What's Still Pending?

Although completing Compiler Design feels good, I still have some work to finish.

The notes for all Compiler Design lectures are pending. I also have DBMS work and notes to complete.

With October ending on the 31st, I have very little time left to achieve my monthly target.

My plan is to work on the pending Compiler Design notes and DBMS over the remaining two days.

The target remains the same: complete the entire DBMS subject by October 31.

It's going to require careful time management, especially with the amount of pending note-making.

Day 46: One More Subject Completed!

Thursday wasn't an extraordinary day in terms of routine, but it was certainly a meaningful one for my preparation.

I completed Compiler Design, a subject that initially seemed intimidating because I had never studied it before.

More importantly, I came away with greater confidence in my ability to approach unfamiliar subjects.

There are still notes to prepare, DBMS to finish and plenty of revision ahead. But today, I am happy to mark another subject as completed.

Compiler Design: Completed!

Now, it's time to deal with the remaining October targets.

One subject at a time.
