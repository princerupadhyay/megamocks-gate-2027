Day 31 of my GATE CSE 2027 prep · 14 October 2026

COA Progress and Slowly Clearing the DBMS Backlog

Feeling: good, unhurried, enjoying the studies
Computer Organization and Architecture Database Management Systems

Today was a pretty good day. I completed the Control Unit chapter in Computer Organization and Architecture (COA), watched one lecture on floating-point representation, and continued studying the ER Model in DBMS.

The other important part of today was the DBMS backlog I have been trying to clear since Sunday.

I'm not completely caught up yet, but I'm making progress. And for now, that feels manageable.

What I Studied Today

COA: Control Unit Completed

I completed the third chapter of COA, which covers the Control Unit.

Along with that, I completed one lecture on floating-point representation.

Floating-point representation is another important topic in Computer Organization and Architecture, so I'm continuing to work through it as part of my existing schedule.

The Control Unit chapter is now complete, which is a good addition to my COA progress.

DBMS: Continuing the ER Model

I also completed the second lecture of the ER Model chapter in DBMS.

The ER Model was supposed to be covered on Sunday, but things didn't go according to the original plan. As a result, some of its lectures became part of my backlog.

Instead of treating the chapter as something entirely separate from the backlog, I'm using my current study sessions to work through those lectures.

This means the work I'm doing now serves two purposes: it helps me continue with DBMS and gradually reduces the lectures I missed earlier.

Understanding My Sunday DBMS Backlog

My original Sunday backlog contained six DBMS lectures.

Two were related to concurrency control, specifically lock-based protocols. The other four belonged to the new ER Model chapter.

Over yesterday and today, I worked through two concurrency-control lectures and two ER Model lectures.

That leaves four lectures pending:

  • Two ER Model lectures.
  • Two concurrency-control lectures.

So, the backlog hasn't disappeared yet. But at least it has become smaller.

I'm trying to manage this without immediately shifting my entire study schedule by a day. For now, I want to see how much I can recover alongside the subjects already planned for the week.

Is the Backlog Going to Affect My Schedule?

Honestly, I don't know yet.

Today, the study phase felt relatively light, and I was able to make progress without feeling that I had to push myself excessively.

I'm also enjoying my studies at the moment, which makes the process feel more manageable.

However, I know that a lighter day doesn't guarantee that every upcoming day will be equally comfortable. As I continue with the schedule, I'll have to see whether the remaining DBMS lectures begin interfering with my other targets.

My goal is still to complete the syllabus by 30 October. After that, I want to devote the remaining preparation period to revision, question practice, mock tests, and analysis.

I don't want to ignore the backlog, but I also don't want to make the entire schedule unnecessarily rigid before I know whether that is actually required.

For now, I'll continue with the existing plan and adjust if the situation demands it.

Calling It a Day

Once I had finished today's work, I decided to stop studying and go to sleep.

There wasn't much point in forcing another session just for the sake of doing more. I had completed the planned COA progress for the day and made further progress on DBMS.

Sometimes, the sensible thing is to finish the work you've done and get proper rest instead of trying to squeeze every possible minute out of the day.

What I Realized on Day 31

I'm learning to distinguish between having a backlog and being completely off track.

I still have four DBMS lectures left from Sunday, and I need to make sure they don't interfere with my October syllabus-completion target. At the same time, I'm continuing to study COA and DBMS rather than abandoning the current plan to recover everything at once.

For now, the workload feels manageable, and I'm enjoying the process.

Whether that remains true as the schedule gets heavier is something I'll discover over the next few days. I'm not going to pretend I already have the perfect solution.

Day 31 is complete. The Control Unit chapter is done, floating-point representation is underway, and the DBMS backlog is getting smaller. Tomorrow, I'll continue with the schedule and see how things unfold.
