Day 6 of my GATE CSE 2027 prep · 19 September 2026

A Weekend Without Studying

Feeling: a mix of relaxation and regret

Not every day of GATE preparation is going to be productive. Sometimes, despite having a plan, things don't go the way I expected. Saturday was one of those days.

I didn't study anything for GATE CSE 2027. I spent most of my time with my girlfriend, enjoyed the day, and came home late. And somewhere between enjoying my personal time and thinking about my preparation, I ended up feeling a little regretful.

What Happened on Saturday?

Saturday was already planned as a day to spend time with my girlfriend. We went out and enjoyed our time together.

Since I was away from home, I didn't have access to my study notes. By the time I came back home, it was already late, and I didn't feel like studying.

My books were there, but I wasn't really in a position to make meaningful progress.

So, that's pretty much it. No lectures, no questions, no PYQs, and no test analysis.

Just a day away from GATE preparation.

The Weekend Plan That Didn't Happen

I had planned to use Saturday and Sunday to catch up on my daily question practice.

My target was to solve at least five questions from each completed subject or topic, as far as possible across the weekend. I also wanted to work on my pending notes, particularly in Theory of Computation (TOC).

On top of that, I had my second subject-wise C Programming test scheduled for Sunday.

The idea was to use the weekend to make up for the practice I hadn't managed to complete during the week.

But Saturday didn't go according to that plan.

And honestly, that bothered me.

Enjoying My Day While Regretting Not Studying

The interesting thing about Saturday was that I felt two different things.

On one hand, I was enjoying my time with my girlfriend. We had already planned the outing, and I wanted to spend that time with her.

On the other hand, whenever our conversation went quiet, the thought of GATE preparation would come back into my mind.

I would start thinking that it was Saturday, I should be studying, and I was missing an opportunity to make the weekend productive.

It was a mix of relaxation and regret.

I wanted to enjoy my personal time, but I also wanted to make progress towards my goal of getting a single-digit rank in GATE CSE 2027.

And those thoughts kept coming back.

Should I Have Forced Myself to Study?

Honestly, I don't think forcing myself to study would necessarily have helped.

I wanted to study after coming home, but I didn't feel like I could concentrate properly. I felt that if I forced myself to sit with my books, I might end up wasting time without actually understanding anything.

If I wasn't mentally ready to study, I thought it would be better to rest rather than pretend to be productive.

At the same time, I can't completely ignore the fact that I regretted not studying.

I don't think either feeling cancels out the other.

I enjoyed my day, and I still wish I had studied.

Both things can be true.

Weekends Are Important for My GATE Preparation

Since I'm balancing my corporate job at TCS with GATE preparation, weekends are particularly important to me.

On weekdays, I have to manage my preparation around my work schedule. That makes weekends valuable opportunities to catch up on questions, revise completed subjects, work on pending notes, and take tests.

When the weekend doesn't go as planned, I feel like I've missed an opportunity that I might not easily get back during the working week.

That's what made Saturday a little frustrating.

I had a plan, but I wasn't prepared for how difficult it would be to fit studying around an outing that had already been decided.

I also didn't have my notes with me, which meant I couldn't easily use any spare time for preparation.

What I Could Have Done Differently

Looking back, I think I could have prepared better for the day.

Since I already knew I was going out, I could have considered carrying some notes or planning a small amount of revision that I could do while away from home.

That doesn't mean I should have sacrificed the entire outing for GATE preparation.

It simply means that I could have thought about how to balance both things before leaving.

I didn't do that this time, and Saturday ended up being a complete break from studying.

What I Realized Today

Saturday reminded me that my GATE preparation doesn't happen in isolation.

I have a job, personal commitments, and other things happening in my life. Sometimes, these things take up the time I had originally planned to use for studying.

I don't want to pretend that every weekend will be perfectly productive.

But I also don't want to keep feeling that my weekends are slipping away without making progress.

The important thing for me is to acknowledge what happened and understand why it happened, instead of pretending that I completed tasks I never actually did.

Tomorrow

I'll discuss Sunday's preparation separately tonight.

For now, Saturday is recorded for what it was: a day spent enjoying personal time, with no GATE study and some regret about not making the weekend as productive as I had hoped.

Day 6 complete.
